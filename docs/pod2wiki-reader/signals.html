Jason Lemkin / @jasonlk / engagement 11

A year ago our AI agents took Amelia and me about 30 minutes a day, combined. This last week it was eight hours a day. Each. Three humans, 20+ agents, and we’re busier than we were with a 20 person team. Everyone assumes that means the agents got worse. They got dramatically better. That’s the reason. When an agent can only run a task, you check the task. When an agent can make decisions, you need an opinion about every decision it makes. And it makes a lot of them, all day, some of which it never mentions to you. A year ago when they failed, they failed loudly. They errored, they stopped, you knew. Now they hand you something that looks finished. Verifying “looks finished” is slower than fixing “obviously broken.” The other half is that they have enough context to want things. Every morning I log into 10K, our AI VP of Revenue, and it has new things it wants to build. Not tasks to run. Things to build. It’s not facetious at this point to call it a team member who raises their hand every single day with a new idea. Anyone who’s run a software team knows that feeling. I had 200 years of good ideas at EchoSign we never built, and my CTO used to mock me for it. Great idea Jason, that’s a 2030 feature. The difference now is the constraint moved. It used to be engineering capacity. Now it’s my attention, and my attention doesn’t scale with model releases. The eight hours aren’t overhead from bad agents. They’re the cost of managing really good ones. https://t.co/Byr0bhmgGD
