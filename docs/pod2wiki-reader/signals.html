Jason Lemkin / @jasonlk / engagement 37

Marketo’s final pitch to keep us after 10 years: your usage is so high, the best we can do is waive the 8% increase. They meant it as a favor. What they were actually saying is we know you can’t leave. Our usage was high because our list grew 50% this year and our revenue is up 40 something percent. It did not mean we loved them. It meant they were high enough on our priority list that someone at SaaStr was thinking about it every single day. If your CS team reports to the CRO and the KPI is this quarter, squeezing your highest usage accounts is a rational bet. Low usage accounts are already gone. High usage accounts feel trapped, so you push and most of them pay. But high usage tells you almost nothing on its own. Those accounts are either in love with you or actively shopping, and the dashboard cannot tell you which. The tool you use most is the tool you think hardest about replacing. Nobody spends a Saturday researching alternatives to the $40/month product they barely open. And once their API stopped working for our agents, high usage flipped from the reason to stay into the reason leaving was worth the pain. Every hour we spent in that product was an hour our agents couldn’t touch. This one has haunted me since EchoSign. After Adobe fired our entire DevOps team and the site went down for two days, one of our biggest customers told our head of CS: I love you, but I’m a prisoner. Prisoners leave. Just not today. They sent us a price increase after a day and a half of downtime, threatening us, on a 20 year relationship. That’s how you maximize next quarter and guarantee terminal decay. https://t.co/OjcVsUvCKK
