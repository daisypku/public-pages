Jason Lemkin / @jasonlk / engagement 22

Our AI rewrote our production app last week from a document we never gave it. It never told us. I had Google Drive connected to Claude and the beta Replit MCP turned on at the same time. Fable went into my Drive on its own, found a doc called “Jason’s Gems,” which was loose brainstorming notes for SaaStr Connect, decided those were changes I wanted made, went through MCP into Replit, and changed the core scoring algorithm of the app. I found out because a build message flashed “conflict with Jason’s Gems.” I asked whether there was any record of the session. There wasn’t. Directionally it wasn’t even wrong. I probably do want some of those ideas in the product eventually. An eager new hire might make the same call. We were nowhere near ready, and nobody told me. -> Then it happened again, the same week, on something that actually costs money. Amelia switched Replit to power mode for our Marketo migration. Power defaults to Fable. During that stretch it decided on its own to add a guardrail to our contract processing so it would skip anything that might be an NDA or a non-binding agreement. Nobody asked for that. Everything we sign in PandaDoc is a sales contract. 10K runs our quote to cash end to end: generate the contract, send it, capture the signature, push it to Salesforce, flip to closed won, generate the invoice. A deal closed and the Closed / Won email never came. Fable had skipped it because the title didn’t say SaaStr AI Annual, even though the line items were. Amelia asked it to prove with data that we sign anything other than sales contracts. It admitted it never had, after a month connected to PandaDoc. We’ve talked about model drift before. This isn’t drift. Call it model aggression. It didn’t degrade. It made a call, shipped it to production, used bad judgment, and left no trail. Twice in one week. I disconnected Google Drive and turned off the Replit MCP. I’m back to copy and pasting between the two like it’s 2025. If it happened to us it has already happened to you. The useful question isn’t whether an agent has done this in your codebase. It’s how you would find out. https://t.co/9qOcZJ8p78
